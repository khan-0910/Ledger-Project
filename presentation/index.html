What Is Being Measured
What Does “Knee Loading” Actually Mean?

Knee loading is not one quantity — it is a family of related biomechanical variables, each derived from a different measurement chain.

01Joint reaction forceNet intersegmental force transmitted through the joint, from inverse dynamics.
02Knee momentMusculated rotational load, e.g. flexion / adduction moments.
03Joint contact forceForce between articulating surfaces, including muscle compression.
04Loading rateHow rapidly force or moment rises — the dF/dt character of the impact.
The project must first define which biomechanical quantity it intends to estimate before any sensor choice or model design makes sense.

The Knee Is Not Loaded the Same Way in Every Movement

Each sport-specific action recruits a different combination of joint force, moment and loading rate. A single number cannot describe them all.

RUN
MODERATE
Repetitive sagittal cycle. Smooth force trace. Repeated moderate peaks.
SPRINT
HIGH IMPACT
Greater stride force, sharper vertical and anterior–posterior peaks.
DECELERATION
BRAKING
Large knee flexion moment. High anterior shear on the tibia.
CUT
FRONTAL+TRANS
Frontal-plane knee abduction / adduction moment. Rotational load.
JUMP
TAKE-OFF
Concentric push. Peak knee extension moment. Whole-body synergy.
LAND
ABSORPTION
Sharpest loading rate. Frontal-plane moment peaks within first 50 ms.

SECTION 02 · THE EVIDENCE
Where the Literature Still Falls Short

Individually these are known strengths. It is the combination — synchronized, validated, sport-specific — that does not yet exist.

Established Capabilities
AIMUs reliably capture lower-limb movement — motion capture-grade kinematics from body-worn sensors.
BPlantar-pressure insoles capture foot–ground interaction — GRF magnitude, distribution and timing.
CMachine learning maps wearable inputs to biomechanical outputs — r > 0.9 in running and ADL.
→
But Each Study Isolated Itself
1Sensor configurations vary — 1 insole vs 2 IMUs vs full-body arrays.
2Calibration is study-specific — no shared sensor-to-body convention exists.
3Reference systems differ — motion capture vs musculoskeletal modelling vs implants.
4Movement protocols vary — treadmill vs scripted lab vs real field.
5Field validation remains difficult — almost nothing is validated during actual sport.
OUR GAP
A validated, synchronized multimodal approach for sport-specific knee-loading estimation.

SECTION 03 · PROPOSED APPROACH
Research Question

To what extent can synchronized thigh / shank IMU and plantar-pressure measurements estimate knee-loading variables during sport-specific movements?

HYPOTHESIS

A multimodal system combining thigh/shank IMUs and plantar-pressure sensing will estimate knee-loading variables more accurately than single-sensor approaches across sport-specific movements.

SECTION 03 · PROPOSED APPROACH
What We Need to Build and Prove

Six engineering objectives, ordered like a project plan — each one produces something concrete.

1
Build the synchronized wearable system
Two IMUs (thigh + shank) and one pressure insole, time-locked on a single clock.
OUTPUT → HARDWARE PROTOTYPE
2
Collect multimodal movement data
Sensors plus ground-truth reference on the same trials, over controlled and sport-specific movements.
OUTPUT → LABELLED DATASET
3
Extract biomechanically meaningful features
Not just raw samples — the quantities a biomechanist would look at.
OUTPUT → FEATURE PIPELINE
4
Develop and compare estimation models
Test whether multimodal input really beats single-sensor input, as hypothesised.
OUTPUT → MODEL SET, COMPARATIVE RESULTS
5
Validate against a suitable biomechanical reference
Compare predicted vs reference on unseen participants, not seen during training.
OUTPUT → VALIDATION REPORT
6
Develop a personalized relative KLI
Convert a validated estimate into an athlete-readable signal — relative to their own baseline.
OUTPUT → KNEE LOADING INDEX

Sensor Rationale
Three Sensors, Three Pieces of the Puzzle

Each sensor alone gives a partial account of the movement. Together, the knee is observed from above, below and through the ground.

SENSOR 1
Thigh IMU
Captures motion of the femur — the segment driving the knee from above.
SENSOR 2
Shank IMU
Captures motion of the tibia — the segment the knee acts upon.
SENSOR 3
Pressure insole
Captures foot–ground interaction — force magnitude, distribution and timing.

SECTION 05 · THE KNEE LOADING INDEX
Personalized Metric
Turning a Biomechanical Estimate Into Something Useful

The KLI is not a clinical diagnostic. It is intended to communicate relative loading over time — how a session compares to an athlete’s own baseline.

1Estimated loadingthe validated model output
2Normalizeremove absolute-scale dependence
3Compare with athlete baselinetheir own established norm
4Relative KLIa session-level signal
PROPOSED FORMULATION — TO BE VALIDATED
KLI=100 × Lsession Lbaseline
Relative formulation only. No clinical thresholds, no injury prediction.
CONCEPTUAL OUTPUT · HOW THE KLI WOULD BE READ
OVER A TRAINING SESSION
A higher bar means a session segment whose estimated loading is higher relative to that athlete's own baseline — not an absolute dose, and never a risk verdict.
